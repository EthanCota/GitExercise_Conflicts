## 2. Letterform description (descriptive only)

- **Capital "S" (first name):** a single continuous spiral stroke — a tight
  curl at the top opening into a large bowl that sweeps down to the baseline,
  then a short forward hook connecting directly into the following "a." No pen
  lift.
- **Connectivity:** "Sara" is written with full cursive ligatures — every
  letter connects to the next at or near the baseline with no pen lifts
  visible. The surname's first three-to-four letterforms are likewise
  connected to each other and to the flourish that follows.
- **Capital initial of surname:** a tall, rigid, nearly vertical downstroke —
  visually distinct from the "S" in that it shows almost no curvature and a
  much smaller entry hook. It is proportionally the tallest single stroke in
  the whole signature, extending noticeably higher above the baseline than the
  "S" does.
- **Descender loop:** a large, open oval loop originates at/near the base of
  that capital downstroke, sweeps down and to the left, does **not** fully
  close (there is a visible gap between the two ends of the oval at its
  lower-left), then the stroke crosses back over its own upward path — creating
  a visible X-crossing below the "or" letters — before flattening out and
  continuing rightward as the terminal thread. Because no letter in the
  legible portion of the name (Sara Lor-) structurally requires a descender
  this large, the loop reads more as an **added decorative flourish (paraphe)**
  than as the necessary descender of a specific letter — though if the
  surname's initial is in fact "J" (which does carry a natural descender),
  the loop's position is at least consistent with that letter's stroke path.
- **Stroke-width contrast:** the loop and the terminal thread are visibly
  **thinner** than the strokes forming "Sara" and "Lor-". This is a genuine,
  measurable contrast, not an artifact of the crop — it holds throughout the
  loop and the exit thread. Plausible explanations (not distinguishable from
  the image alone): reduced pen pressure/faster hand speed during the
  flourish, a different or fine-tipped writing instrument for the flourish, or
  the flourish having been added as a distinct pass.
- **Terminal stroke behavior:** the last portion of the surname shows
  progressive simplification — legible letter humps give way to a smooth,
  nearly straight, slightly undulating line with no further letter-specific
  curvature. This pattern (name written out, then trailing into an
  unarticulated thread) is typical of a practiced, rapidly executed signature
  where the writer no longer forms discrete letters at the end.
- **Relative letter heights:** lowercase letters in "Sara" and "Lor-" occupy a
  consistent x-height band; the two capitals (S, and the surname initial) both
  extend well above that band, with the surname's capital reaching
  noticeably higher than the "S."
- **Writing instrument:** the main letterforms show **uniform stroke width**
  with **blunt, rounded stroke terminals** (e.g., at the top of the "S" and at
  the top of the surname's capital downstroke) and no tapering into fine
  points — consistent with a **felt-tip pen or marker** rather than a ballpoint
  (which typically shows width variation and hard, uniform-width tapering) or
  a fountain pen (which typically shows ink pooling/feathering at stroke
  starts). The visible width difference between the bold name letters and the
  thin flourish/thread (above) is the one departure from strict uniformity.
- **Substrate:** the background shows a fine, uniform speckled/mottled texture
  and a soft overall lighting gradient (subtly darker toward the upper portion
  of the frame) rather than a flat, evenly-lit scan. There is no glare/specular
  highlighting or ink-skipping typical of dry-erase marker on a whiteboard, and
  no visible grid lines or fibrous paper texture at this resolution either.
  The most consistent reading is a **close-up phone photograph of a plain
  light gray/blue paper or card surface** under ordinary (not studio) lighting,
  though a smooth whiteboard cannot be fully excluded from this crop alone. A
  faint, very low-contrast circular smudge is visible lower in the full frame
  (roughly mid-page); it is too faint to interpret and may be a shadow,
  reflection, or a pre-existing faint mark on the substrate.

## 3. Provenance of the image itself

The image is an **iOS/iPadOS Photos-app screenshot**, not a direct camera
export. Evidence:

- **UI chrome burned into the pixels:** a back chevron (top-left), a "Crop"
  pill button (top-right, indicating the Photos app's edit/markup mode), an
  ellipsis "more options" control, and an info pill reading **"Folsom Area —
  Today  9:35 AM."**
- **That info pill is the Photos app's own rendering of the underlying
  photo's metadata (reverse-geocoded location + capture time), displayed as
  ordinary pixels on screen** — it is data *about* the photo shown as an
  on-screen label, not metadata embedded in this file. It cannot be extracted,
  verified, or cross-checked the way real EXIF/GPS tags could be; it is exactly
  as trustworthy as any other pixel in the picture (i.e., it proves nothing by
  itself and could in principle be altered like any other visual element).
- **Embedded file metadata was checked directly** (EXIF + XMP are present in
  this PNG): `UserComment = "Screenshot"`, `DateTimeOriginal` /
  `DateCreated` = **2026-08-27T18:58:14**, no GPS tags, no camera make/model.
  This confirms the file itself self-identifies as a screenshot, and the
  timestamp recorded is the **screenshot capture time (evening, 18:58)** —
  which does **not** match the "9:35 AM" burned into the on-screen info pill.
  That mismatch is expected and instructive: the two timestamps describe two
  different events (when the underlying photo was taken, vs. when the Photos
  app screen showing that photo was screenshotted) and only the latter is
  actually recorded in this file's metadata.
- The file is a **16-bit RGB PNG carrying an Apple ICC profile**, which is not
  the native format most iPhone screenshots are captured in — this indicates
  the image has been **re-encoded/processed at least once** (e.g., by the
  upload/ingestion pipeline that produced the file examined here), so any
  fine-grained noise/texture assessment above should be treated as
  post-processing-affected, not a direct read of the original camera or
  screenshot bytes.
- **What this closes off:** no camera make/model, no lens data, no original
  GPS coordinates, no original-capture EXIF timestamp, and no way to verify
  the "Folsom Area / 9:35 AM" claim independently — all of that information,
  if it ever existed, belongs to an *original photo* that is not the file in
  hand. **No metadata-based authentication of the photographed document, its
  location, or its time of creation is possible from a screenshot alone.**
  Resolution has also plainly been reduced relative to whatever the original
  photo was (the photo now occupies less than half the frame height, cropped
  by on-screen chrome and further degraded by at least one screenshot +
  re-encode cycle), and the fine speckled texture visible under magnification
  is at least partly attributable to this compression/re-encoding chain rather
  than necessarily to the substrate itself.

## 4. Limits

This examination rests on a **single image of a single signature instance**,
with **no exemplars** (no other samples from the same hand) available for
comparison, and the image itself is a **multiply-degraded screenshot** (photo
→ Photos-app render → screenshot → at least one re-encode). Concretely, this
rules out: (a) any authenticity or forgery determination, which requires
comparing multiple genuine exemplars for natural variation ranges — one
instance cannot establish or refute that; (b) any writer-identification claim
beyond what is legibly written, since no reference signature exists to match
strokes against; (c) any personality/graphological inference, which is not a
validated method regardless of sample quality; (d) precise measurement claims
(stroke-width ratios, letter-height ratios, angle measurements) beyond
relative/descriptive comparison, since the image has undergone unknown scaling
and compression; (e) substrate identification with certainty (paper vs. board)
beyond a best-fit description; and (f) any authentication of the location/time
overlay, since it is rendered pixel data from an intermediate app display, not
extractable metadata, and the file's own embedded metadata only documents the
screenshot event, not the original photograph.
