## B. Constraint Model — Binding Rules Checklist

Citations are to `issuer-rules.json` unless noted. Key structural fact used throughout this plan: **Chase's 5/24 rule counts personal cards from any issuer; it explicitly exempts business cards** ("Denies applications if applicant has opened 5+ personal credit cards... in past 24 months... Business cards (Chase Ink) do NOT count toward 5/24" — Chase/5/24 rule). This is why business cards are used below as slot-free plays.

**Chase**
- [ ] 5/24: personal-card openings from **any** issuer count; AU accounts count; clock runs from open date, not closure date (Chase/5/24 rule).
- [ ] Business cards (Ink) don't count toward the 5/24 tally, **but** applicant must still be under 5/24 at time of application to be approved for a Chase business card (Chase/5/24 rule) — so business-card windows must close before the personal count reaches 5.
- [ ] As of 2026-01-29, 5/24 is auto-denied pre-human-review — no exceptions to bank on (Chase/5/24 enforcement).
- [ ] Sapphire bonus rule changed 2026-01-22: 48-month rule eliminated; now once-per-lifetime **per card product**, Preferred and Reserve tracked separately — both are independently earnable even while holding both cards (Chase/Sapphire bonus eligibility).

**American Express**
- [ ] Once-per-lifetime bonus per card product, personal and business tracked separately (Amex/once-per-lifetime).
- [ ] Max 5 Amex cards open at once, personal + business combined (Amex/1-5 card limit).
- [ ] Max 2 new Amex approvals per rolling 90 days (Amex/2-90 velocity) — applies across personal and business.
- [ ] Pop-up jail: an ineligible-for-bonus warning before submitting; typical sentence 60–120 days of "good behavior," reports of 1+ year if application pattern doesn't change (Amex/pop-up jail).
- [ ] Green → Gold → Platinum family rule: opening Platinum kills Gold/Green bonus eligibility permanently; opening Gold kills Green but not Platinum (Amex/family rule). **Sequencing consequence:** once Platinum is taken, Gold/Green bonuses are gone for good.
- [ ] 2026 enforcement escalation extended the once-per-lifetime language explicitly to Green and Gold (previously looser) (Multi-issuer/2026 enforcement escalation).

**Citibank**
- [ ] 48-month bonus reset per product; clock starts when bonus **posts**, not when approved (Citi/48-month bonus rule).
- [ ] Max 1 Citi approval per 8 days (Citi/1-8 velocity).
- [ ] Max 2 Citi approvals per 65 days, stacks with the 1/8 rule (Citi/2-65 velocity).

**Bank of America**
- [ ] 2 cards/30 days, 3/12 months, 4/24 months — all three apply simultaneously; business cards exempt (BofA/2-3-4 velocity rule).

**Capital One**
- [ ] Venture and Venture X share one 48-month bonus-eligibility family; clock starts on bonus **post** date. Moving "down" the ladder (Venture X → Venture) triggers the rule; moving "up" does not. Business Venture X is exempt (Capital One/Venture family 48-month rule). **Relevant to downgrade planning in §E.**

**Wells Fargo**
- [ ] No new WF consumer card within 6 months of the last one (excludes Bilt Mastercard, Signify Business Cash) (Wells Fargo/6-month rule).
- [ ] 48-month reset on bonus/intro-APR/fee-waiver for the **same product**; clock starts on card open date (added Aug 2024) (Wells Fargo/48-month intro bonus rule).

**Barclays**
- [ ] Loose/inconsistent 6/24 rule — approvals above 6/24 are commonly reported; softer than Chase's (Barclays/6-24 rule, Medium confidence).
- [ ] Same-card bonus re-eligible after 24 months (Barclays/24-month bonus eligibility, Medium confidence).

**US Bank**
- [ ] 48-month reset on same personal-card-product bonus; business cards exempt (US Bank/48-month bonus rule).

**Velocity interactions to plan around**
- [ ] Never stack a Citi app inside 8 days of another Citi app, or a 3rd Citi app inside 65 days.
- [ ] Never open >2 Amex cards in a rolling 90-day window.
- [ ] Never let 5 Chase-countable personal-card opens land inside a trailing 24-month window unless intentionally accepting a Chase lockout.
- [ ] Business cards' bureau-reporting matters separately from 5/24-counting: Chase/Amex/BofA/Citi/Wells/US Bank business cards do **not** report to the personal bureau; Capital One and Discover business cards **do** (except Cap One Spark Cash Plus / Venture X Business, which don't report routinely) (Multi-issuer/business card personal credit reporting; Discover/business card personal credit reporting). This affects utilization visible to other lenders, separate from the 5/24 count itself.

## C. Sequenced 24-Month Plan — Baseline (2/24 used, 740+ score, S = $3,000/mo)

**Core logic:** (1) Chase-first while under 5/24, because Chase is the only issuer that locks you out of *both* its personal and business products once you cross 5/24 [`issuer-rules.json`: Chase/5/24 rule]. (2) Grab the Chase **business** card (doesn't consume a personal slot) *before* the personal-slot count reaches 5, since business-card approval still requires being under 5/24 at application time. (3) Only one min-spend clock runs at a time at S=$3,000/mo, because even the cheapest 3-month-window card in the table (Southwest Biz, $1,000/mo) plus any other clock would generally exceed $3,000/mo combined — **CALC**: e.g. Ink Biz Preferred alone needs $2,667/mo, leaving only $333/mo of slack, not enough to run a second clock concurrently. (4) Amex Business Platinum is **excluded** from this baseline: its $20,000/3mo min spend needs $6,667/mo, more than double S — infeasible without spend acceleration (see §D, §E).

## D. Sensitivity

**S = $1,500/mo (half of baseline).** The $2,000+/mo cards (Ink Biz Preferred $2,667/mo, Sapphire Reserve $2,000/mo, Amex Platinum $2,000/mo) no longer close organically inside their windows — CALC shortfalls of $1,167/mo, $500/mo, $500/mo respectively. Two responses:
1. **Re-sequence toward low $/mo-needed cards first:** United Explorer ($833/mo), Citi Strata ($1,333/mo), Bilt ($1,333/mo), Delta Reserve ($1,500/mo, exactly at the S ceiling) all fit organically; front-load these, push Ink Biz Preferred/Sapphire Reserve/Amex Platinum later or skip them.
2. **Where a gap is small, use the tax-payment bridge (see §E) only if the card's cited cpp clears the processor fee.** At S=$1,500, Sapphire Preferred's shortfall is $167/mo — a ~$500 tax-payment top-up at Pay1040's 1.75% [`spend-and-risk.json`: "Legitimate Spend - Federal Tax Payments"] costs **CALC** $8.75, against a 2.05 cpp card [`bonuses.json` note] — net positive by 0.30 pts/$, clearly worth it for a small top-up.
Net effect: expect ~4–5 completed cards in 24 months instead of 6–8, EV total roughly proportionally lower (CALC, driven by which subset is chosen), not linearly half — because the highest-EV/lowest-$/mo cards (Amex Gold-adjacent Platinum) are prioritized over volume.

**S = $6,000/mo (double baseline).** Two concurrent clocks become feasible whenever their combined $/mo needed ≤ $6,000 — e.g. Ink Biz Preferred ($2,667/mo) + Sapphire Reserve ($2,000/mo) = $4,667/mo, comfortably under S, cutting elapsed time for those two cards roughly in half versus running them sequentially (still respecting the rule of never running >1 concurrent clock unless S covers both, per the task's constraint). This also brings Amex Business Platinum into range: $20,000/3mo needs $6,667/mo, a **CALC** $667/mo ($2,000 over the 3-month window) shortfall even at S=$6,000 — small enough to close with one estimated-tax payment (e.g. $2,000 via Pay1040 at 1.75% = **CALC** $35 fee) against a **CALC** $4,905 proxy-valued EV (§A) — clearly worth doing if the fee-math and risk profile (§E) are acceptable. Total slot count from §B is still capped at 3 new personal cards regardless of spend velocity — 5/24 is a hard count, not spend-gated — so higher S compresses **time-to-bonus**, and unlocks Business Platinum's massive min-spend, but doesn't let you hold more personal cards inside the same 24-month/5-24 window.

**Starting at 4/24 instead of 2/24.** Only 1 personal slot remains before hitting 5/24. Chase-first logic now means: get the business card (Ink Biz Preferred) *first* while still under 5 (at 4/24, satisfies "under 5"), **then** spend the single remaining personal slot on the higher-EV Sapphire Preferred ($1,342 EV) rather than Reserve — this reaches 5/24 and permanently forecloses further Chase approvals (personal or business) until an old slot ages off. Sapphire Reserve is simply not available in this scenario without foregoing something else. **CALC marginal cost vs. baseline:** losing the second Chase personal slot means forgoing Reserve's $1,135 EV (or substituting one non-Chase personal card of similar EV, e.g. Citi Strata at $1,060, largely offsetting the loss). Everything after the Ink+Preferred pair (Amex Platinum, Citi Strata, Bilt, Cap One Venture X) proceeds unchanged, since none of those issuers check Chase's 5/24.

**Cliff logic (distinct from the per-dollar breakeven above):** because a sign-up bonus is a step function (you get it all or none), a small top-up at a "bad" per-dollar rate can still be a great trade if it's the difference between hitting and missing the whole window. Example (**CALC**): $500 short with a week left on a window worth a $2,565 EV card (Amex Platinum) — even Plastiq's 2.99% ($15) is trivial against $2,565 at risk. Always compare the fee on the *marginal shortfall only* against the *entire bonus EV*, not against the whole min-spend.

**Tax notes:**
- Bonuses earned via a spending requirement are treated as a rebate, not taxable income ("Tax Treatment - Spend-Required Bonuses"); this covers every card in this plan.
- A bonus paid with **no** spend requirement is taxable misc. income, and issuers must 1099 amounts of $2,000+ starting the 2026 tax year (up from $600) — but all taxable income must be reported regardless of whether a 1099 is issued ("Tax Treatment - No-Spend Bonuses"; "Tax Treatment - 1099-MISC Threshold 2026").
- **Referral bonuses, inference (CALC):** `spend-and-risk.json` does not use the word "referral" anywhere, but a referral bonus by its nature requires no personal spend threshold from the referrer — applying the file's own no-spend-bonus rule, referral payouts should be treated as taxable misc. income subject to the same $2,000 1099-MISC threshold. This is an inference from the cited general rule, not a direct citation — flagged as such.
